Privacy

Most privacy policies are long because the company has a great deal to explain. This one is short for the opposite reason. Here is exactly what happens to your data.

Your shows stay in your browser

The list of shows you track is saved in your own browser's local storage. It is never stored on the server, and there is no account to sign into — no email, no password, no “create a free profile to continue”. Clear your browser data and it is gone for good, including from my side, because I never had a copy.

What the server sees

To work out runtimes and build your statistics, your browser sends the logged shows to the server, which looks up episode lengths, does the arithmetic, and sends the results straight back. That list is used to answer the request and then discarded. It is not written to disk and not stored against you.

Search results and seasons lengths are cached here for seven days, purely so I am not hammering the TMDB API. Without it, the hundredth person to type Breaking Bad today would have me knocking on TMDB's door for the hundredth time to be told, once again, that it ran for five seasons. That cache holds the search text and its results and nothing else — no visitor information is attached to it, so a cached search cannot be traced back to whoever typed it.

Cookies (there aren't any)

Showtime sets no cookies. No consent banner, no “we value your privacy” pop-up concealing four hundred advertising partners behind a “legitimate interest” toggle. There is genuinely nothing to consent to.

You don't have to take my word for it. Open your browser's developer tools and look.

The usage counts we do keep

I keep a small pile of totals, so I can tell whether anyone is actually out there: visits per day, how many shows get added, searches performed, and a tally of which shows and genres are logged most often. These are plain numbers. There are no per-person records sitting behind them.

You only count as a visitor once you are actually tracking something. Merely opening the page doesn't do it — which conveniently keeps crawlers and uptime bots out of the figures.

To count daily visitors without identifying anyone, your IP address and browser user-agent are mixed with a random secret and hashed into a meaningless string. That secret is regenerated every day and the old one is destroyed. So the hash cannot be reversed, and tomorrow you are a completely different meaningless string. I could not follow you from one day to the next if I wanted to. The hashes themselves are deleted once the day has been counted; only the total survives.

No third-party tracking

No analytics services, no advertising networks, no tracking pixels, no social media widgets. Nothing about you is sold, shared or handed to anyone — largely because there is nothing to hand over.

Your browser does load a couple of files from other hosts:

  • themoviedb.org — show artwork and metadata
  • cdnjs.cloudflare.com — the Font Awesome icon set

Those hosts see the request your browser makes for the file, as they would on any site with external assets. Neither is told anything about what you track. The confetti, you will be relieved to hear, is served from here.

Opting out

If your browser sends a Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control signal, you are left out of the visitor counts entirely — no hash is calculated for you at all. Most of the web quietly ignores both. I would rather not.
